\title{A cross-domain collection of spatial and spatio-temporal datasets for statistical modelling}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent\bfseries
Spatial regression methods are commonly evaluated through rich simulations
but limited empirical evidence. In our review of 101 articles, the median was
one real dataset per article and 27 used none. Reusable spatial data remain
dispersed across papers, software and repositories, with inconsistent
documentation of spatial structure. We present a cross-domain spatial data
bank built from these sources. It comprises 276 technically ready datasets and
analysis-ready variants derived from 129 sources, spanning agriculture,
ecology, climate, hydrology, health, natural hazards, housing and social
science. Each dataset or variant contains a response, predictors and a
resolved formula; 272 declare a coordinate reference system. The bank covers
point, polygonal and gridded source supports. Standardized artefacts may use
representative points or centroids, while original areal geometries are
retained separately when available. Reproducible loaders preserve provenance,
transformations, temporal and parent relationships, and distinguish
author-provided spatial weights from project reconstructions. The bank also
defines reproducible subsampling and semi-synthetic constructions on real
supports. The final distributed collection will be fixed after source-level
licence and redistribution audits.
\normalfont
\end{abstract}

\section*{Background \& Summary}

Simulation studies are central to the development of spatial estimators
because they provide a known data-generating process and controlled variation
in sample size, dependence, neighbourhood structure, noise and coefficient
heterogeneity. Empirical applications provide a different form of evidence:
they expose methods to measurement processes, missingness, spatial supports,
temporal structures and covariate distributions that are difficult to encode
in a finite simulation design. A purposive review assembled during the
construction of this resource covered 101 spatial-method articles with an
explicit response, at least one covariate, a parametric data-generating
process and repeated Monte Carlo evaluation. Although these articles
contained 199 coded simulation experiments and at least 2,977 documented
parameter cells, 27 used no real dataset and 62 used only one. The median was
one real dataset per article. This descriptive contrast motivated the
collection of a broader and more traceable empirical resource; it is not
presented as an estimate of field-wide practice.

Existing data infrastructures solve complementary parts of this problem.
PMLB provides uniform access and machine-readable metadata for tabular data,
whereas OpenML distinguishes datasets from fully specified tasks, suites and
runs \cite{Romano2022Pmlb,Bischl2021Openml}. Collections such as TableShift
and TabReD demonstrate that a partition represents a scientific target, such
as transfer across domains or prediction forward in time, rather than a
purely technical setting \cite{Gardner2023Benchmarking,Rubachev2025Tabred}.
Spatial data resources including CAMELS-US, LamaH-CE, Caravan and LAGOS-NE
provide strong models for documenting geometries, supports, temporal coverage
and variable provenance
\cite{Addor2017Camels,Klingler2021Lamah,Kratzert2023Caravan,Soranno2017Lagos}.

Data Descriptors that consolidate heterogeneous sources also show the need to
separate the original resource from the standardized object that is actually
distributed. MedMNIST v2 reports source-specific transformations, file
contents and partitions; the Compound Matrix-Based Project Database links
source collections to common representations and automated checks; and the
Construction Motion Data Library records the transformations required to
align structures, units, coordinates and labels
\cite{Yang2023Medmnist,Kosztyan2024Cmpd,Tian2022Construction}.

The present collection applies these principles to cross-domain spatial and
spatio-temporal data. Each released entry identifies the statistical unit,
response, predictors, source model, spatial support, time variables and
available geometry. Information about spatial weights distinguishes matrices
distributed by the source authors, matrices reconstructed from a documented
rule and matrices created for a new project analysis. Longitudinal sources
remain connected to their parent panels rather than being represented only by
independent cross-sections. The collection therefore provides both reusable
data artefacts and the information needed to judge whether a proposed model,
weight structure or evaluation split is consistent with the source.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Scope and unit of description}

The collection distinguishes a \emph{source dataset} from an
\emph{analytical record}. A source dataset denotes the data resource released
by its original producer. An analytical record denotes a response,
predictor set, formula, period or scientifically meaningful subset associated
with that source. Several analytical records may therefore point to one
source dataset without being counted as independent data collections.

Only records that satisfy the release criteria are described as members of
the published collection. Inclusion requires a recoverable data artefact, an
identified source, a defined response and predictor set, sufficient spatial
information for the stated use, a documented transformation path and a
completed technical review. Records awaiting source verification, manual
review or redistribution decisions remain in the internal catalogue and are
not counted in the released total.

\subsection*{Source discovery and acquisition}

Sources entered the project through three chronological routes. The first
route examined datasets distributed by R and Python packages and used package
documentation, object metadata and cited publications to identify their
origin. The second searched data repositories and portals for reusable
spatial resources. The third began with methodological articles and traced
their empirical applications to repositories, supplementary material,
software packages or data providers. This article-first route complemented
the repository search when papers referred to data that were difficult to
locate through metadata alone.

Original downloads are retained unchanged whenever redistribution and storage
conditions permit. A source manifest records the access location, retrieval
date, original filename, citation and available version information. Derived
artefacts are produced by separate loaders so that acquisition and
transformation remain distinguishable.

\subsection*{Methodological-literature audit}

The literature audit used DOI as the principal deduplication key and retained
regression studies with an explicit response, at least one covariate, a
parametric DGP and repeated Monte Carlo regeneration. Pure kriging, universal
kriging and regression-kriging studies were outside the quantitative scope.
For each included article, coding distinguished independent DGP regeneration
from bootstrap resampling, randomization and MCMC. Simulation experiments,
parameter cells, methods, metrics, basic design factors and qualitative
misspecification axes were recorded with supporting passages from the full
text. Cell totals were classified as exact, lower bounds or
non-reconstructible.

Real datasets were counted once per article and source, even when several
outcomes, formulas, periods or subsets were analysed. Validation of the real
application was coded separately from validation used only in simulation.
Among the 74 articles with at least one real dataset, 59 used only in-sample
analysis, 13 combined in-sample and out-of-sample evaluation, and two used an
exclusively out-of-sample design. The corpus was assembled to diversify
methodological families and publication periods rather than to estimate the
prevalence of these practices in the complete literature.

\subsection*{Evidence extraction and source fidelity}

Article PDFs were converted to structured TEI XML with GROBID to support
search and extraction. Tables, equations, figures and passages for which the
TEI representation was incomplete were checked against the rendered PDF.
Extracted claims were treated as evidence requiring verification rather than
as authoritative metadata.

For every released analytical record, the review identifies the response,
covariates, sample definition, unit of observation, study period and model
formula used in the source article when these are reported. The source
formula and a locally executable formula are stored separately when file
names or available variables require an adaptation. A local formula is not
presented as the authors' model unless it is supported by the article or its
replication material.

\subsection*{Data conversion and harmonisation}

Dataset-specific loaders convert source files into stable analytical
artefacts. Conversion may include parsing, type normalization, joins,
variable renaming, documented filtering and construction of geometry from
published coordinates or identifiers. Each loader records the input files,
output files and transformations applied. Raw files are not overwritten.

Harmonisation is limited to the information required for consistent access.
Original units, coding choices and variable names are retained in provenance
metadata even when standardized names are exposed to users. Missing values
are not silently imputed. When a paper analyses a subset of a larger public
resource, the released record either reproduces that subset from documented
rules or states that the available artefact differs from the authors'
analytic sample.

\subsection*{Spatial and temporal representation}

Spatial support is recorded as point, areal unit, grid, network or another
source-defined structure. Geometry metadata include the source geometry,
coordinate fields, coordinate reference system and any transformation applied
by the loader. Operations requiring distance are not validated from coordinate
presence alone; the coordinate reference system must be suitable for the
stated calculation or an explicit transformation must be documented.

Temporal data retain stable unit and time identifiers. A panel is represented
as a parent dataset with its longitudinal structure intact. Cross-sectional
or period-specific derivatives remain linked to that parent and record their
selection rule. These relations make it possible to prevent the same unit or
derived observation from entering incompatible training and evaluation sets.

\subsection*{Spatial-weights provenance}

Spatial weights are described by their origin, unit order, construction rule,
neighbour definition, normalization and treatment of units without neighbours.
Three cases are distinguished. A \emph{source-author matrix} is an artefact
provided or explicitly used by the authors. A \emph{reconstructed matrix}
implements a rule reported in the source. A \emph{project matrix} supports a
new analysis chosen by this project. A project matrix is never attributed to
the source authors, even when it uses the same observations.

\subsection*{Metadata and registry construction}

Each released record is linked to a machine-readable registry entry and a
human-readable dataset description. The registry identifies the source,
artefact, response, predictors, formula status, spatial support, temporal
structure, geometry, coordinate reference system, weights provenance, parent
relations, licence evidence and validation state. Stable identifiers connect
the source dataset, analytical records and distributed files. The release
manifest additionally records file size and checksum.

\subsection*{Quality-control workflow}

Automated checks verify schema conformance, referenced paths, variable names,
formula terms, identifiers, dimensions and parent--derivative relations.
Spatial checks examine geometry validity, coordinate reference systems and the
correspondence between weight matrices and observation order. Longitudinal
checks examine the uniqueness and completeness of unit--time identifiers.
These checks identify inconsistencies but do not promote a record
automatically. Final inclusion requires review against the source article,
documentation and available data artefacts.

\subsection*{Ethics and sensitive data}

The collection reuses data released by their original producers. For sources
involving human participants, animals or sensitive locations, the dataset
record cites the source study's ethics and access statements and preserves any
restrictions on redistribution. The present release does not replace the
conditions imposed by the original data provider.

\subsection*{Record-level metadata}

For each analytical record, the registry exposes its stable identifier,
source identifier, parent dataset when applicable, response, predictors,
published formula, executable formula, statistical unit, spatial support,
time fields, geometry reference, coordinate reference system, spatial-weights
reference, provenance class and validation state. Dataset-level descriptions
provide definitions, units, transformations, missing-value conventions,
known differences from the source analysis and recommended citations.

\subsection*{Relationship between source and derived records}

Derived records do not replace their source dataset. Period-specific slices,
subsamples and other transformations retain the source identifier, parent
identifier, transformation rule and random seed when randomness is involved.
The final Data Records table will report source datasets separately from
analytical or derived records so that the size of the collection is not
inflated by multiple representations of the same resource.

\section*{Technical Validation}

\subsection*{File integrity and schema validation}

Every deposited file will be checked for readability, expected dimensions,
file size and SHA-256 checksum. Registry entries will be validated against the
published schema, and every referenced artefact will be required to resolve
within the archived release or to an explicitly documented external source.

\subsection*{Variable and source-model checks}

Responses and predictors will be compared with the variables present in the
released artefacts. Formula terms, sample sizes and study periods will be
reviewed against the source article or replication material. Deviations needed
to make a model executable will be reported as local adaptations rather than
silently substituted for the published specification.

\subsection*{Spatial validation}

Spatial validation will test geometry type, missing and invalid geometries,
coordinate reference system declarations and identifier uniqueness. For
distance-based uses, the validation record will state the coordinate system
in which distances are computed. Spatial-weights checks will cover matrix
dimensions, observation order, normalization, symmetry when expected and
units without neighbours. Provenance will be checked separately from numerical
validity.

\subsection*{Temporal and parent--derivative checks}

Panel records will be checked for stable unit and time identifiers, duplicate
unit--time pairs and consistency between parent datasets and derived slices.
Where partitions are distributed, the validation will ensure that grouping or
chronology required by the intended prediction problem is preserved.

\subsection*{Loader reproducibility}

Released loaders will be executed in a clean environment from the archived
source files whenever redistribution permits. Their outputs will be compared
with the deposited artefacts using dimensions, schemas and checksums or
documented tolerance rules. The final manuscript will report the number of
records passing each check and list any validation that cannot be performed.

\section*{Usage Notes}

Users should begin with the registry and the associated dataset description
rather than selecting files by filename alone. The description identifies the
response, permissible predictors, statistical unit, geometry, time fields and
known differences from the source analysis. RDS files can be read with
\texttt{readRDS}; GeoPackage files require a spatial reader such as
\texttt{sf::st\_read}.

Spatial weights must be matched to the released observation identifiers and
their order before analysis. Reconstructed and project matrices should be
cited and described according to their own construction rule. For panels,
users should retain unit and time identifiers and prevent related slices from
crossing evaluation partitions. Random, spatially blocked, temporally ordered
and new-region splits answer different questions and should not be treated as
interchangeable.

Users should cite the original data creators in addition to this collection.
The registry records do not override source licences or access restrictions.
